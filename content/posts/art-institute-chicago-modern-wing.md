## The Flying Carpet: Daylighting as Material

Visiting the Modern Wing on an overcast afternoon reveals something direct sunlight obscures: the ceiling louvers diffuse northern lake light into a matte, shadowless wash. In contemporary exhibition spaces, artificial fixtures often dominate with high-CRI halogen pin-spots that create dramatic hot spots. Piano’s design does the inverse—it prioritizes ambient sky coherence over dramatic pin-lighting.

## Spatial Rhythm & Movement

The gallery sequencing moves visitors from dense, intimate alcoves into broad, double-height volumes overlooking Millennium Park. This contrast resets the eye. When viewing large-scale abstract expressionist canvases or mid-century bronze sculptures, the eye requires breathing room between intense color fields.

### Curatorial Observations

1. **Pedestal Elevation**: Sculptural pieces are mounted at true sightlines without reflective vitrines, allowing full 360-degree inspection of patinas and chisel marks.
2. **Acoustic Dampening**: Despite high ceilings and terrazzo flooring, acoustic absorption behind wall fabric prevents the echoing flutter common in monumental galleries.
3. **Pacing**: Benches are positioned with sightlines aligned toward architectural axes rather than crowded directly in front of major canvases.
